# The verification kernel

The kernel is the part of QED that decides, by fixed rules, whether money may move. It is a component of the wallet,
not the wallet (QED_CONTEXT non-negotiable 1). It has one entry point, `shared/kernel/index.ts`, and every caller
reaches it through that module.

Status: research prototype. Not externally audited. Local chain and test tokens only.

## Boundary

Inside the kernel: pure rules. Each is a function of its arguments only. None reads a clock, a database, a chain or
the network; none calls a model; none uses randomness. Money is counted in exact integers wherever the kernel says so
below.

| Part | Modules | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| Plan checks | `shared/kernel/x5.ts` (Checker A), `shared/kernel/checkerB.ts` (Checker B) | Whether a household plan may be signed: no stated scenario ends worse than doing nothing |
| Certificates | `shared/kernel/coverage.ts` and its internal modules | Guarantee or abstain on a reserve, principal or CVaR request |
| Authority budget | `chain/rail/authority.ts` (R7 decision D7), `chain/rail/earned-authority.ts` (K1) | How much the agent may move out of the workspace's control, and how much of it was earned |

Outside the kernel: everything that turns records into the rules' inputs, and everything that acts on a verdict.

| Caller | Its adapter (outside the kernel) | Kernel functions it calls |
|---|---|---|
| The server | `server/programs.ts`, `server/household.ts`: database rows to plans and models | `exactCheck`, `checkerB`, `X5_DEFAULT`, `PLAN_KEYS`, `ZERO_PLAN` |
| The lab | `server/lab.ts`: the household's books to certificate requests | `checkCoverage`, `dayNum`, `isoOf` |
| The co-signer (check C12) | `chain/rail/authority-ledger.ts`: reads the release's events, classifies them as the rule's events, replays them, prices an action | `AuthorityBudget`, `EarnedLedger`, the fraction helpers |

The co-signer's engine (`chain/rail/cosigner-engine.ts`, generated by `chain/scripts/fork-cosigner.mjs`) imports the
ledger adapter, and the adapter imports the kernel through the entry point. The payment preview
(`chain/rail/treasury.ts`) and the escrow's job view (`chain/rail/escrow.ts`) use the same adapter, so preview,
enforcement and display are one computation.

Signing, keys, the vault contract and the owner account are outside the kernel. A verdict is enforced only where a
signer or a contract acts on it.

## Inputs and outputs

| Function | Inputs | Output |
|---|---|---|
| `exactCheck(plan, s0?, model?)` | a plan (five amounts, rupees), the household's balances, an `X5Model` | `X5Verdict`: `rejected`, `reasons`, `tierD`, scenario outcomes |
| `checkerB(plan, s0, model?)` | the same, read independently | `CheckerBVerdict` |
| `checkCoverage(request)` | a request in the wire format (integer minor units, ISO currency, calendar dates) | `CoverageOutcome`: a level (certified by construction, certified contractual, bounded under model, abstain), reasons, remedies, the request's hash |
| `new AuthorityBudget(params, C, P, { facts? })` and its events (`pay`, `convert`, `hold`, `settleHold`, `releaseHold`, `credit`, `uncredit`, `reversal`, `fee`, `grant`) | B0, r, k; the control set C and principal set P; events in base units | the budget, what is available, and a log in which every change is recorded |
| `new EarnedLedger(cap, r)` with `sent(to, amount)` and `credit(id, payer, amount, time)` | the caps (window L, X_w, X_c), r; transfers out of C; final settlements | each settlement's credit, with what netting and the caps kept from it |

## Guarantees

Each guarantee holds under the assumptions stated with it, and each cites its evidence.

1. Same inputs, same output. The kernel has no hidden state across calls (the classes keep only the state their own
   events built).
2. The entry point changes nothing. Every name it exports is the very object its defining module exports
   (`shared/kernel/index.test.ts`).
3. Plan checks: Checker A matches the X5 Python checker on 255 fixtures to within ₹0.006; Checker B matches the
   independently written X7 checker on 255 fixtures (`x5.test.ts`, `checkerB.test.ts`). Model parameters are
   illustrative.
4. Certificates: the checker never returns a level weaker than every level the request accepts; money is integer
   minor units, and a float where money belongs is refused (`coverage.test.ts`, owned by the certificates work).
5. Authority budget (D7): the budget rises only by credits, reversals and owner grants; conversions and self-payments
   never raise it; facts mode replays history as it happened (`chain/test/authority.test.ts`, the reference's 400
   sequences and 3,000 seeded sequences).
6. Earned authority (K1, Theorem K1 in `docs/research/qed-math-formal/20261124-k1-earned-authority.md`): a credit is
   never more than D7 gives; in any window of L seconds of chain time at most X_w is earned from all payers together
   and X_c from any one payer address; money the workspace sent an address does not count when that address pays it
   back (what counts is the high-water mark of the address's net contribution). Proved in writing; checked by Z3 for
   every history of up to six events with symbolic amounts, times and caps; property-tested on the implementation
   (`shared/kernel/earned-authority.test.ts`).

## What the kernel does not guarantee

- That its inputs are true. The adapters read the database and the chain; a wrong read gives a wrong verdict
  (assumption A-OBS: the co-signer's view of the chain is the chain).
- That a verdict is enforced. The co-signer signs or refuses; the vault contract enforces its own limits.
- Who anyone is. A payer QED cannot link to a member is treated as an outside client (D7 assumption A-ID). Two real
  people paying each other real money cannot be told apart from trade by any rule that sees only amounts, addresses
  and times. The K1 caps bound what that can add: X_w in any window, X_c from any one address.
- That money netted is all the money that came back. Netting sees only transfers of the vault's own asset out of
  the workspace's addresses. One extra hop through an address the workspace never paid, or a conversion through a
  venue, is not seen; the caps are then the whole bound.
- Finality beyond the chain's. A settlement counts after k confirmations (k = 2 on the local chain). On a chain with
  a finality rule, the adapter should read at the finalized block (D-123's proposal; not done).
- Returns, prices or market behaviour. Reference bands are fixed in configuration. A model-bound certificate is
  labelled as not a guarantee.
- Independence from the release. Checks C1 to C11, T1 to T3 and P1 to P3 are the release's engine, outside the
  kernel; only C12's rule is the kernel's.

## Rules for changing it

- A caller outside the kernel imports only `shared/kernel/index.ts`. `index.test.ts` fails on any other import of a
  kernel module (tooling that compares the coverage checker with the Python checker is the stated exception).
- A new export is added to `index.test.ts` with the module it comes from.
- A change to a rule keeps its own fixtures and properties passing, or says in its commit which claim it changes.
